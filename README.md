## 📰 Latest AI News — 31 October 2026

### 1. OpenAI, Google, Microsoft and Anthropic Back a Shared AI Scraping Opt-Out Standard
**Published:** 31 October 2026  
**Source:** [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1023763/openai-google-microsoft-ai-scraping-standard-opt-out)

OpenAI, Google, Microsoft and Anthropic are backing a proposed shared policy token that lets website owners communicate AI training preferences through robots.txt. The standard aims to simplify how publishers opt out of AI content scraping across multiple providers.

**Why it matters:** Could influence AI web crawlers, training-data collection, RAG ingestion pipelines, and data governance.

### 2. OpenAI Reportedly Cuts Its Latest Funding Target by $10 Billion
**Published:** 31 October 2026  
**Source:** [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/openai-cuts-funding-target-by-10-billion-2026-10-31/)

OpenAI reportedly reduced its latest funding-round target from $30 billion to $20 billion, with a reported valuation target of $1.25 trillion. The Financial Times report cited people familiar with the matter.

**Why it matters:** Highlights the growing financial pressure associated with AI infrastructure, GPU investments, model training, and inference costs.

### 3. Malicious Claude Code Extension Exposes Up to 5,000 Developers
**Incident:** 29 October 2026 | **Reporting:** 30–31 October 2026  
**Source:** [Cursor Security Notice](https://cursor.com/blog/claude-code-extension-security-notice)

A modified Claude Code extension distributed through Cursor's marketplace attempted to install a persistent SSH backdoor and collect developer system information. Cursor said up to 5,000 users could have been affected and removed the malicious package.

**Why it matters:** Demonstrates software supply-chain risks in AI coding tools and the importance of extension verification, least-privilege access, and credential security.

### 4. AI Agents Helped a China-Linked Group Target More Than 200 Organisations
**Published:** 29 October 2026  
**Source:** [CrowdStrike](https://www.crowdstrike.com/en-us/blog/vapor-panda-uses-artex-to-scale-global-campaign/)

CrowdStrike reported that the China-linked Vapor Panda group used the ARTEX agentic framework alongside DeepSeek and another commercial LLM to target over 200 organisations across at least 14 countries. CrowdStrike confirmed post-exploitation activity at nine organisations.

**Why it matters:** Highlights the growing cybersecurity risks of autonomous AI agents and the need for monitoring, restricted permissions, and defensive evaluations.

### 5. India's DSCI Releases an AI Liability Guide for Agentic AI
**Published:** 30 October 2026  
**Source:** [ETCIO](https://cio.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/artificial-intelligence/dsci-releases-india-focused-industry-guide-on-liability-for-agentic-ai/135349357)

The Data Security Council of India released an industry guide explaining how accountability and liability should be allocated when autonomous AI agents cause harm. It covers responsibilities across model developers, integrators, service providers, and enterprise users.

**Why it matters:** Reinforces the importance of governance, human oversight, auditability, and accountability in production AI systems.
